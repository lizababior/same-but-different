# About

## What this site is
Same But Different shows two people side by side. You give the two people the same traits, then you change one trait, for example race, gender or age. The site shows how life in the US can be different for each group in seven areas: finishing college, getting hired, getting promoted, equal pay, getting a loan, health care and being recognized by AI.

## How to use it
1. Go to **Explore**. At the start, the two people are the same.
2. Change one trait on one side. For example, select **Woman** for the left person and **Man** for the right person. A trait that only one person has is yellow. On the other side, the same option has a dashed border.
3. Look at the list of life areas in the middle. Select an area to see its facts. If an area says "same for both", the facts in it apply to both people. If you cannot select an area, we have no facts for these traits in it.
4. Read the facts below the list. They are in groups: **Only the left person**, **Only the right person** and **Both**. If there are many facts, scroll the list.
5. Read each fact card:
   - The bold line compares the two groups. "1.8× as likely (6.9% vs 3.8%)" means that the rate for the group was 6.9%, and the rate for the comparison group was 3.8%.
   - **Finding** means a study result that has no two numbers to compare.
   - **Intersection** means that the fact is about a combination of two or more traits, for example Black women.
   - The number in brackets, for example [4], opens the original source.
6. Change more traits to compare more groups. Select **Reset** to start again.

## Why we made it
Data about unequal treatment is often in long reports and in tables that are hard to find. This site puts these facts in one place, so that anyone can compare two groups in a few clicks. We show the numbers and the sources, and you make your own conclusions.
